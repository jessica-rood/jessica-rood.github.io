Working Papers

2026

  1. NBER
    Beliefs, Attention, and Investments in Early Childhood
    Flávio Cunha, Snejana Nihtianova, Jessica Rood, and Anja-Lize van der Merwe
    2026
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    We develop a model of parental investment in early childhood in which bandwidth-constrained parents hold distorted beliefs about the returns to responsive interaction. When capacity falls short of aspiration, motivated reasoning provides relief: the parent distorts her working belief downward, rationalizing low engagement. Distorted beliefs suppress responsive parenting, which generates no calibration evidence, which, in turn, confirms the distortion. The model identifies four channels through which interventions escape this self-sealing trap. A randomized controlled trial confirms the model’s predictions on parental beliefs, measures of responsive parenting, behavioral engagement, and absence of impact on materials or placebo outcomes.

  1. Kinder
    Economic Mobility in the Houston Area, Part 1: An Analysis of the Long-Term Impact of the 2014–2016 Oil Shock on Houston’s Economy
    Hasan Ahamed, Snejana Nihtianova, Joy Njeh, Jessica Rood, Bradley Selsberg, Anja-Lize van der Merwe, and Flávio Cunha
    2026
    Center for Economic Mobility, Kinder Institute for Urban Research, Rice University
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Code

    Using labor-market and income data for 2009–2019, extended through 2024, the report compares the Houston metro area with 54 large metropolitan areas with little dependence on oil and gas. After the 2014–2016 collapse in oil prices, Houston’s income per resident and private employment stayed well below the comparison group, by about 13 and 7 percent on average over 2015–2019. The damage ran mainly through weaker hiring, lower pay for continuing and new workers, and higher job turnover; population growth slowed through lower in-migration rather than a wave of departures.

  3. Kinder
    Economic Mobility in the Houston Area, Part 2: A Proposal of Four North Stars
    Hasan Ahamed, Snejana Nihtianova, Joy Njeh, Jessica Rood, Bradley Selsberg, Anja-Lize van der Merwe, and Flávio Cunha
    2026
    Center for Economic Mobility, Kinder Institute for Urban Research, Rice University
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Proposes four measurable North Stars for the Coalition for Economic Mobility’s goal of improving economic success across the 10-county Houston region by 2040: children grow up out of poverty; children grow up in places that pay a dividend; young people stay connected to school or work; and adults reach economic security. Each pairs an aspiration with a measure built from public national data, so anyone can compute, verify, and compare it across regions; the measures track conditions but do not identify the effects of particular initiatives.

  4. GRH
    Our Youngest Learners, Our Brightest Futures: Understanding Houston Families’ Choices and the Power of Pre-K
    Courtney Durbin and Jessica Rood
    2026
    Good Reason Houston
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    A survey of more than 900 income-eligible families across 11 Houston-area districts, with follow-up focus groups, on why eligible children do not enroll in public pre-K. One in four families had not heard of any pre-K option, while most families with a child still to enroll planned to use one; the report traces the gap to awareness and to understanding of who is eligible, and closes with recommendations for districts.

  1. Inside the Production Function: Turnover and Public Investment in Early Childhood Education
    Jessica Rood, Ji-Hee Lee, Snejana Nihtianova, and Jennifer Bouek
    2026
    Work in progress
    Abs Bib

    This paper examines turnover in the early childhood education workforce using qualitative methods, asking how high staff turnover undermines the very initiatives designed to improve childcare quality. Drawing on 90+ interviews across three Texas-based quality improvement programs—Shared Services Alliances, the Early Educator Registered Apprenticeship Program, and the Pre-K Partnership Expansion Initiative—we identify a vicious cycle in which workforce instability hampers program implementation. Emerging themes include a professional-development paradox, threats to partnership viability, and a mismatch between program design and the realities of ECE careers.

  2. When Free Isn’t Enough: A Stated Choice Experiment to Understand Low Pre-K Enrollment
    Jessica Rood
    2026
    Work in progress
    Abs Bib

    Despite substantial public investment, more than half of eligible children in the Houston region are not enrolled in free public pre-K. This paper asks how families learn about, consider, and choose early care and education—and what knowing the facts would change. In a survey of Houston-area families with children ages one to four, stated-choice screens place every option in front of each family at true or experimentally set attributes, recovering preferences without frictions, while expected and past choices carry the frictions; measured awareness and beliefs separate the two. A randomized information treatment—one bundled fact about cost, hours, eligibility, and sign-up, against a deadline reminder—tests what correct information changes in beliefs and in expected choices at the age-3 and age-4 pre-K transitions. Fielding is planned for spring 2027.
